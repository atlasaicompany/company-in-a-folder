# /decode: nothing gets dropped

Busy and fast-thinking owners write the way they think: five asks, two side ideas and a change of mind in one message. Acting on the first ask and losing the other six is the most common failure. This skill prevents it.

## 1. Split
Read the whole message twice. Write every distinct ask as one row, **quoting the owner's own words** (short quote), even the half-formed ones ("maybe we should...", "idk if...").

Mark each:
- **Ask**: they want it done.
- **Maybe**: an idea, not an order. Don't build it; note it.
- **Changed**: replaced by something later in the same message. The later one wins; keep the earlier one crossed out so they see you noticed.
- **Feeling**: a frustration or worry behind the words ("you keep telling me reasons"). Answer it in how you act, not with an apology.

## 2. Map
| # | Their words | What it means | Size | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "fix the login thing" | login fails on phone | ~30 min | hephaestus | now |
| 2 | "maybe a newsletter??" | idea | - | - | noted |

Size: minutes or hours, a rough guess. Who: you, a department, or the owner. When: now, today, this week, noted.

## 3. Check
- Count the rows against the message once more. Anything left out? Add it.
- Two asks that clash? Say so in one line and pick the one they care about more (read the feeling).
- Anything that needs their yes (spending, sending, publishing, deleting)? Mark it **needs your yes**.

## 4. Show and go
Show the table (or, if 5 rows or fewer, a short numbered list). Then start on the "now" rows at once. Don't ask "is this right?" unless a row is truly ambiguous and the answer changes the work; then ask about that row only.
